About

One lab. Two campuses.

The Predictive Analytics and Technology Integration Lab (PATENT) builds explainable, data-driven AI that turns complex data into predictions people can trust and act on.

Our story

From Starkville to Tuscaloosa.

  1. Mississippi State University

    Founded in Starkville

    PATENT was established in MSU's Department of Computer Science and Engineering, with Dr. Shahram Rahimi as founding director. It was set up as a university-wide hub for data management, integration, predictive modeling, and simulation.

  2. The McKay Endowment

    Built to last

    An endowment from MSU alumnus Lamar McKay, retired Deputy Chief Executive of BP, and Nancy McKay gave the lab lasting support for infrastructure, people, and travel.

  3. The University of Alabama

    Re-established in Tuscaloosa

    When founding members moved to The University of Alabama, they re-established PATENT in the Department of Computer Science, now based in Cyber Hall.

  4. Today

    One research family

    The two labs share students, projects, and papers. PATENT at Mississippi State is directed by Dr. Andy Perkins. At Alabama, PATENT partners with the BRAINS Lab.

Interdisciplinary by design

AI that crosses disciplines.

PATENT aims to accelerate advances across interdisciplinary fields by tackling the challenges of data management, integration, and analytics, as well as predictive modeling and simulation. By integrating technologies and data sources, including artificial intelligence and machine learning, we develop next-generation solutions that are data-driven, resilient, and robust, in both real-world and theoretical domains and across diverse industrial sectors.

Artificial Intelligence

Fundamental and applied AI: deep and reinforcement learning, neuro-symbolic and hybrid AI, knowledge graphs, natural language processing, and explainability.

Cybersecurity

Explainable intrusion detection and response, threat intelligence, and the security of AI systems themselves.

Cyber-Physical Systems

Predictive maintenance, digital twins, autonomous vehicles, robotics, and the Internet of Things.
